How I work

I am a full stack developer. I build SaaS and automation with React, Next.js, Node.js, PostgreSQL and Docker, and I have been working since my first semester at university, without a break. I am reading Software Engineering at UCSAL, finishing in late 2027.

Five clients so far, from law firms to a travel agency, and I run the whole cycle: requirements, architecture, data modelling, integrations, deployment and production support. I would rather ship the flow that solves the client's problem than ship beautiful technology nobody uses.

A good part of the work happens before any code. On my own projects I also handle the commercial end: proposal, pitch and the client relationship. And at JVB I knew nothing about law, so the system came out of talking to the people who live that routine. What stayed standing was what helped their day, not what looked good in my editor.

What interests me most right now is agent orchestration: letting AI take the repetitive triage and hand back to a human only what needs judgement. That is what I am building in production with LangGraph, MCP, RAG and API integrations.

Two things I treat as part of the job and not as decoration: tests and accessibility. The larger system I maintain runs over 3,500 automated tests, with secret scanning blocking every push. This site navigates entirely by keyboard, respects anyone who asked for less motion in their operating system, and passes contrast. You do not have to take my word for it: just press Tab.

And I have broken things in production. A healthcheck made the system learn the wrong public URL and write attachment links pointing at a host that does not exist. I wrote up the whole path, from symptom to root cause, and what changed afterwards: the full incident.
